Touch

A real touch driver.
No kernel extension.

Plugged into a Mac, the Edge is a vague "touch board": taps land in the wrong place. The Toolbox reads the digitizer directly, so a tap lands where you touch, two fingers scroll and pinch, and edge swipes change screens.

When your finger lifts, the pointer goes back to wherever you were working.

Instruments

Gauges that tick, not spin

Forty-eight ticks over 270 degrees, animated on the render server. The whole board idles under one percent of a core.

Up next

Your day, at a glance

The next events from your calendar, with the one happening now lit. Tap for the full agenda.

Running

Every app, one tap away

Tap to bring an app forward. Hold to send its window to another display.

Now playing

The record on the desk

Spotify or Music as an album card with transport and scrubbing, driven by events instead of polling.

Yours

Drag, resize, add

Small, wide and tall tiles on a two-row grid. Hold the board to rearrange it. It stays the way you left it.

Ambient

A night clock when
you're not looking.

Pull down from the top edge and the strip drops to a quiet clock with the next event, the weather and the music. Tap to bring the panel back. Updates install themselves while it rests.

Install in a minute

  1. 1

    Download, move to Applications

    One app in a zip. Notarized by Apple. Nothing runs as root.

  2. 2

    Allow Input Monitoring and Accessibility

    The app asks, opens the exact Privacy pane if macOS won't, and confirms the moment you flip the switch.

  3. 3

    Let it fix the resolution

    macOS often scales the Edge. One tap sets the native 2560 × 720, and undoes it if it looked wrong.
